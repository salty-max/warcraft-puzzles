# Seed revision: world names and playable vocabulary

Date: 1 November 2026. Revision two keeps 100 entries so we can compare the selection
directly with the original seed. All remain evidence-checked research entries, with no
publication approvals.

The original seed leaned too heavily on history books, dragonflight colours and reversed
family relationships. The replacement selection puts more everyday world knowledge
alongside the lore: towns, dungeons, battlegrounds, travel, professions, peoples and an
event.

| Measure                         | Original seed | Revised seed |
| ------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------ |
| Entries / distinct answers      | 100 / 100     | 100 / 100    |
| Multi-word entries              | 1             | 31           |
| Three-letter answers            | 4             | 12           |
| Three- or four-letter answers   | 15            | 20           |
| Ordinary English / proper names | 55 / 45       | 45 / 55      |
| Entries tagged Aspects          | 13            | 4            |
| Evidence records                | 68            | 142          |

Forty entries were replaced. This does not mean all forty were bad answers. Several were
useful individually but crowded the seed with the same subjects. Their earlier versions
remain in Git history for a larger future bank.

## What changed

- Full names now include Dun Morogh, Booty Bay, The Deadmines, Dire Maul, The Dark
  Portal, Argent Dawn, Silver Hand and the three original battlegrounds. We retain
  meaningful spaces, apostrophes and word lengths in display data.
- Short additions include ORE, ELF, ORC, BAT, INN, ALE, PET, IMP, EGG and ORB. These
  supply possible crossings with actual Warcraft clues instead of unrestricted
  dictionary filler.
- Clues were tightened and repetitive family/colour pairs reduced. The CULT clue now
  uses the familiar faction name directly. The PLAGUE and ORB clues make room for a
  little playfulness without inventing facts.
- Hints now use another location, a clearer definition or a useful word cue. Booty Bay's
  hint points to a pirate's plunder. Tarren Mill's hint defines the second word. Short
  answers such as BAT and INN get clear semantic help.
- Client name records preserve exact spelling. They supplement quest/book evidence
  rather than pretending a place label proves its location or history.
- The generated report flags shared source passages for editorial inspection. Those
  overlaps do not automatically mean the underlying facts are identical.

## What the larger-layout numbers mean

By total letter count, 76 answers are eligible for a slot in a 9×9 grid, 87 in 11×11 and
all 100 in 13×13. These figures are upper bounds before crossings, layout and quality
constraints. They are **not successful grid fills**.

The added long names make the selection richer, while the added short words give the
constructor more connecting vocabulary. Only actual fill experiments can establish
whether the combination works. The 5×5 and 7×7 cases remain useful baselines, with 11×11
and 13×13 now included in the planned trials.

## Remaining review targets

- The ratings are still editorial estimates, not calibrated measurements.
- Camp Taurajo and Razorfen Kraul rely on narrower faction/dungeon experience. Verify
  that their clues and crossings make them fair for the target audience.
- Some related clusters remain, including Medivh/Khadgar and Arthas/Frostmourne. A bank
  may contain alternatives; a daily should avoid repeating the same fact or printing one
  answer inside another entry's clue.
- The passages are still all eligible by Vanilla. This revision does not claim complete
  TBC/Wrath coverage, which requires its own source audit.
- No grid has yet demonstrated that this 100-answer seed is enough for dense
  construction. Expand the reviewed bank and benchmark before promising a daily.
